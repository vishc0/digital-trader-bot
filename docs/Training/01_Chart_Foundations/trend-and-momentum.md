# Trend and Momentum for Chart-Based Trading

[Training Hub](../README.md) | [Section Index](./README.md) | [Technical Deep Dive](../02_Technical_Deep_Dive/README.md)

Trend tells you direction. Momentum tells you force.

## Trend Fundamentals

- Uptrend: price making HH/HL.
- Downtrend: price making LH/LL.
- Trend quality: consistency of directional pushes vs pullback depth.

## Momentum Fundamentals

Momentum is the speed and persistence of price movement.

Signs of strong momentum:

- Consecutive impulse candles.
- Shallow pullbacks.
- Closing near candle highs (bull trend) or lows (bear trend).

Signs of weakening momentum:

- Impulse legs shrinking.
- Pullbacks getting deeper.
- More frequent long wicks against trend.

## Pullback Quality Framework

Healthy pullback in uptrend:

- Pullback is slower than prior impulse.
- Volume contracts during pullback.
- Reversal candle appears near support.

Weak trend warning:

- Pullback is fast and high-volume.
- Prior swing low breaks.

## Continuation vs Reversal

Continuation odds improve when:

- Trend and momentum align.
- Pullback is controlled.
- Trigger candle forms at key level.

Reversal odds improve when:

- Structure breaks.
- Momentum diverges and fails repeatedly at extension.
- Rejection candles appear at major higher-timeframe levels.

## Scenario 1: Momentum Continuation

- ETF in uptrend prints strong impulse.
- Pullback forms 3 small red candles with low volume.
- Bullish engulfing at moving average zone.
- Continuation entry has favorable odds.

## Scenario 2: Momentum Exhaustion

- Stock makes new high with long upper wick and no follow-through.
- Next two candles fail to break high.
- Breakdown below local support starts corrective move.

## Execution Notes

- In strong trend, prioritize continuation setups.
- In weak/transition trend, reduce size and demand more confirmation.
- Do not use momentum without structure context.

## Training Drill

Replay 50 charts and classify each impulse leg:

- Strong continuation
- Weak continuation
- Exhaustion

Then compare outcomes by category.

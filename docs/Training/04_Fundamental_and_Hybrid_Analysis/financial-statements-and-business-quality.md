# Financial Statements and Business Quality

[Training Hub](../README.md) | [Section Index](./README.md) | [Fundamental Analysis](fundamental-analysis-for-traders.md)

This module teaches how to read statements like a trader, not an accountant.

## The 3 Statements in Trading Context

1. Income statement: growth, margins, earnings quality.
2. Balance sheet: debt risk, liquidity resilience.
3. Cash flow statement: cash reality vs accounting profit.

## What Matters Most for Traders

### Revenue Quality

- Stable or accelerating top-line growth is usually trend supportive.
- Growth from one-off items is less reliable.

### Margin Direction

- Expanding gross and operating margins often support re-rating.
- Margin compression can precede chart underperformance.

### Cash Flow Integrity

- Strong operating cash flow confirms business health.
- Persistent negative free cash flow increases fragility risk.

### Balance Sheet Health

- Debt-to-EBITDA, interest coverage, and liquidity ratios matter.
- Higher leverage increases sensitivity to rate shocks.

## Business Quality Framework (Quick Score)

Score 0-2 each:

- Revenue consistency
- Margin trend
- Cash flow quality
- Balance sheet safety
- Management guidance credibility

Higher score names are better swing candidates when chart setup is valid.

## Scenario 1: High-Quality Compounder Pullback

- Revenue and FCF trend up for several quarters.
- Temporary chart pullback to support.
- Bullish trigger has higher probability of continuation.

## Scenario 2: Weak Balance Sheet Breakdown

- Debt high, coverage falling.
- Earnings miss and guidance cut.
- Support break has higher odds of sustained downside.

## Red Flags

- Rising receivables with flat cash conversion.
- Large earnings beats with weak operating cash flow.
- Repeated guidance reductions.

## Practical Integration

Use financial quality as a pre-filter before technical screening.

- Quality + bullish chart -> prioritize long setups.
- Fragile fundamentals + weak chart -> prioritize shorts or avoid longs.

# ETF Fundamental Framework (Composition, Flows, Regime)

[Training Hub](../README.md) | [Section Index](./README.md) | [Macro Drivers](macro-drivers-for-stock-and-etf-traders.md)

ETF trading improves when you understand what drives the basket under the chart.

## Core ETF Drivers

1. Holdings composition and concentration.
2. Sector and factor exposures.
3. Macro sensitivity (rates, inflation, growth, dollar).
4. Fund flows and rebalance dynamics.

## Composition Analysis

Key questions:

- Top 10 holdings concentration?
- Sector weight drift over time?
- Market-cap bias (large vs mid vs small)?

A highly concentrated ETF can behave like a few mega-cap stocks.

## Factor Lens

Classify ETF tilt:

- Growth, value, quality, momentum, dividend, low-volatility.

Factor regime determines relative performance behavior.

## Flow Dynamics

- Persistent inflows can support trend continuation.
- Persistent outflows can pressure rallies.

Flow is not a trigger but strong context for swing direction.

## Scenario 1: Growth ETF in Falling-Rate Regime

- Macro backdrop supports long-duration assets.
- Growth ETF breadth improves.
- Pullback setups in trend often perform better.

## Scenario 2: Small-Cap ETF in Tightening Conditions

- Funding costs rise and risk appetite weakens.
- Relative strength deteriorates.
- Breakout quality drops and failures increase.

## Integration with Charts

- Use ETF fundamental profile to set directional bias.
- Use chart structure for entries and invalidation.
- Confirm with volume and breadth behavior.

## Practical Checklist

Before ETF trade:

1. What drives this ETF fundamentally?
2. Is current macro regime supportive?
3. Are flows aligned with chart direction?
4. Is setup quality high on chart?

If fundamentals and chart conflict, reduce size or wait.

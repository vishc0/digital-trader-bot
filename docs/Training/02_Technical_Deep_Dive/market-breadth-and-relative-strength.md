# Market Breadth and Relative Strength (Chart-Focused)

[Training Hub](../README.md) | [Section Index](./README.md) | [Foundations](../01_Chart_Foundations/README.md) | [Execution and Risk](../03_Execution_and_Risk/README.md)

For ETF and stock traders, this topic fills a critical chart gap: not all breakouts are equal unless they are supported by broader participation and leadership.

## Why This Matters

A stock can look good in isolation but fail if:

- Its sector is weak,
- The index is deteriorating,
- Breadth is collapsing.

Relative strength and breadth improve selection quality.

## Relative Strength (RS) Basics

Relative strength asks: Is this instrument outperforming its benchmark?

Examples:

- Stock vs sector ETF (e.g., NVDA vs SMH)
- Stock vs broad index (e.g., AAPL vs SPY)
- ETF vs benchmark (e.g., QQQ vs SPY)

## Practical RS Chart Methods

### Method 1: Ratio Chart

- Plot ratio: Instrument / Benchmark.
- Rising ratio = outperformance.
- Falling ratio = underperformance.

### Method 2: Comparative Swing Behavior

- If market makes lower low but stock holds higher low, that is relative strength.
- If market rallies but stock cannot reclaim resistance, relative weakness.

## Market Breadth Basics

Breadth tells how many stocks are participating in a move.

Common breadth gauges:

- Advance/Decline line
- % of stocks above 20/50/200 MA
- New highs vs new lows
- Sector participation map

## Breadth + Candlestick Integration

- Breakout candle in stock with expanding index breadth -> higher continuation odds.
- Beautiful bullish candle while breadth weakens -> greater fakeout risk.

## Scenario 1: ETF Breakout with Breadth Confirmation

- SPY breaks resistance on strong daily candle.
- A/D line confirms new high.
- Multiple sectors participate.
- Interpretation: move has broad support, follow-through probability improves.

## Scenario 2: Stock Long with Relative Strength Filter

- Two stocks show same bullish engulfing setup.
- Stock A has rising RS ratio vs sector ETF.
- Stock B has falling RS ratio.
- Prefer Stock A; odds and momentum quality are typically better.

## Scenario 3: Divergence Warning

- Index grinds to new high.
- Breadth indicators make lower highs.
- Rejection candles start appearing at resistance.
- Interpretation: underlying participation is thinning; risk of pullback rises.

## ETF-Specific Breadth Notes

- Broad ETFs (SPY, QQQ, IWM) are strongly impacted by internal breadth quality.
- The same candlestick pattern behaves differently in strong vs weak breadth regimes.

## Execution Use Rules

- Use RS as a selection filter before entry.
- Use breadth as a confidence filter for index/ETF direction.
- If candle signal conflicts with breadth and RS, reduce size or skip.

## Weekly Training Drill

For your top 10 watchlist names each week:

1. Rank RS vs sector and index.
2. Mark current chart structure quality.
3. Trade only top-ranked names with valid setups.

This improves focus and cuts low-quality trades.

## Professional Summary

Candles give local signal.
Relative strength gives comparative edge.
Breadth gives market participation context.

Using all three sharply improves ETF/stock chart decision quality.

Volume answers: how much participation backed the move?

## Why Volume Matters

Price can move on low commitment or high commitment. Volume helps distinguish both.

- High volume breakout: stronger sponsorship.
- Low volume breakout: higher failure risk.

## Core Volume Concepts

- Relative Volume (RVOL): current volume vs historical average.
- Climax volume: unusually high volume near move exhaustion.
- Volume contraction: often seen in pullbacks and consolidations.
- Volume expansion: often seen at breakouts and momentum ignition.

## Price-Volume Relationships

1. Price up + volume up: healthy bullish participation.
2. Price up + volume down: weaker upside conviction.
3. Price down + volume up: aggressive selling pressure.
4. Price down + volume down: pullback may be corrective, not impulsive.

## Volume by Setup Type

### Breakouts

- Want expanding volume on breakout candle and at least one follow-through bar.

### Pullbacks in Trend

- Healthy pullback often shows lower volume than impulse leg.

### Reversals

- Reversal candles at key levels become stronger with above-average volume.

## Scenario 1: High-Quality Breakout

- ETF compresses under resistance for 2 days.
- Breakout candle closes above level with 2x average volume.
- Retest holds with moderate volume.
- Continuation probability improves.

## Scenario 2: Volume Climax Trap

- Stock prints huge bullish candle after extended rally.
- Volume is extreme but next candle fails to continue.
- Reversal risk rises as late buyers are trapped.

## Intraday Session Considerations

- First and last hour typically carry higher institutional participation.
- Midday often has lower quality volume and more noise.

## Advanced Tools (Optional)

- Volume Profile (HVN/LVN)
- Anchored VWAP
- Cumulative Volume Delta (platform dependent)

## Execution Rules with Volume

- Do not chase weak-volume breakouts.
- Prefer entries where candle structure and volume agree.
- If expected volume expansion does not appear, reduce conviction.
